Dispatch collection · April – September 2026

Eight readings from the unmonitored island.

A community sensor logged four hundred and sixty-nine days of Bali's breath before it fell silent. A single government monitor reports intermittently for 4.4 million residents. Since the first of April, seven tons of waste a day have been pulled from Denpasar's rivers. The smoke in the alleys rises without an instrument to record it. The science underneath, the practical mask guide, what would actually help, in the order it should be deployed — and now, ninety days into the burn season, the first full data review: what the network actually recorded — followed by what four months of residents worked out for themselves about living with the smoke.

    Field data · 17 April – 20 July
    Ninety days of data on the island's garbage fires.
    The register's first full review — twenty-plus live sensors, roughly two thousand sensor-days of PM2.5, the first dry season under the new waste rules. The season runs about a fifth dirtier than last year at the one sensor able to compare, and the smoke keeps house hours: peaking at nine in the morning, everywhere at once. The signature of a chore, not a harvest.
