Dispatch · IX
How to report a fire, and stay safe doing it.
Filed · October 2026
Approx. 6-minute read

Open burning of household waste is illegal in Indonesia. Official complaint channels exist, they are free, and one of them lets you report anonymously. This dispatch explains how to report in a way that is most likely to stop the smoke.

§ I · The basisWhat the law says.

National Law 18 of 2008 on Waste Management prohibits burning waste in a way that does not meet technical standards for waste processing (Article 29). In practice, that covers a household or business burning its rubbish in a pile, a barrel or a pit. The regency governments and the Province of Bali are responsible for enforcing it, alongside their own regulations. Bali's provincial waste regulation (Perda 5/2011) and Badung's (Perda 7/2013) repeat the ban, with penalties of up to Rp 50 million or a short custodial sentence.

Enforcement depends almost entirely on complaints. Environment officers rarely patrol. They act on reports. A single well-documented complaint in Gianyar in early October brought a field inspection within days, no-burning banners at the reported spot from the village government, and a written reply from the regency environment agency promising follow-up monitoring.

That outcome is not typical. In a community survey of 501 residents carried out in October 2026, 192 people said they had contacted a local authority about burning. Only 38 of them said any action followed. A report is a reasonable first step, not a guarantee.

§ II · Before you reportTalk first, if you safely can.

Many households burn because their collection service has stopped, costs more than they can afford, or will not take mixed rubbish. A complaint does not change that. Where you already have a friendly relationship, a quiet conversation can sometimes do more than a report. Ask what happens to their rubbish and what would help. Some residents have stopped a neighbour's burning by paying for collection and buying bins, together with a calm explanation of the health effects.

Leaves and garden waste can be composted. Seasonal rice-straw burning is a farming practice with its own pressures, and is better addressed through the farmer group (subak) or the village than through a complaint about an individual.

Burning that is repeated, involves plastic or mixed rubbish, or comes from a business or construction site is the clearest case for a formal report. Construction sites and businesses can pay for collection.

§ III · The evidenceWhat a strong report contains.

  1. Where. An exact location. A map pin, plus the banjar and desa name if you know them. Officers cannot act on "near the big temple".
  2. When. Dates and times, more than once. Keep a short log over a week or two. "Burns most mornings around 6 a.m." is far stronger than a single incident. Use a camera app that stamps the date, time and location on each photo.
  3. What. Photos or a short video of the fire and the material. Show what is burning, such as plastic, mixed rubbish or building waste, and the smoke drifting toward homes or a road. Film the fire, not people's faces, and only from a public road or your own property.
  4. Impact. One or two plain sentences. Describe who is affected: children, elderly residents, a school, a clinic, a busy road. If you have a doctor's note about smoke-related symptoms, mention it. A screenshot of a nearby sensor on the live monitor can show how bad the air was, but a sensor cannot show where smoke came from. Do not present a reading as proof against a particular house.

§ IV · The channelsWhere to send it.

  1. LAPOR!, the national public complaints service. File at lapor.go.id, through the SP4N LAPOR! app, or by SMS to 1708. You need to register once. Every report is routed to the responsible agency, which has to reply, and you can track its status. Two options protect you. Anonymous (Anonim) hides your identity from the agency you are reporting to and from the public. Confidential (Rahasia) hides the contents of the report from the public. For a complaint about a neighbour, choose both.
  2. The regency environment agency (DLH). Each regency's environment agency takes complaints directly. LAPOR! reports about waste are usually forwarded to the same agency. In Badung, that is the Environment and Sanitation Agency (DLHK Badung): dislhk.badungkab.go.id, telephone 0361 900926, office hours Monday to Thursday 09:00–15:00 and Friday 08:00–12:00. In other regencies, search for "DLH" and the regency name. The provincial environment agency (DKLH Bali) takes complaints at infodklh@baliprov.go.id.
  3. Emergencies, when a fire is spreading. If a fire threatens buildings, power lines or dry vegetation, call the fire service on 113 or the national emergency number 112. This is a safety call, not a complaint.
  4. The community burn map. Logging a fire with the community burn report adds it to a public record of where and when burning happens, alongside the sensor data. It is not an official complaint, so send one through the channels above as well.
  5. The banjar or desa, with care. The banjar can adopt and enforce a local no-burning rule (perarem) at its monthly meeting, which makes it the most lasting fix. It is also the most personal channel. It works best when the request comes from, or with, a Balinese neighbour, landlord or colleague. Frame it as a health concern for the whole banjar, not a complaint about one family.

§ V · A templateA complaint you can copy.

This letter has been used successfully. Replace the bracketed parts, attach your evidence, and send the Indonesian version. The English is for reference.

Yth. Bapak/Ibu,

Saya ingin melaporkan adanya pembakaran sampah rumah tangga, termasuk sampah plastik, secara terbuka dan berulang di [LOKASI].

Pembakaran tersebut terjadi secara rutin di lokasi tersebut, terakhir pada [TANGGAL dan JAM]. Asap yang dihasilkan menyebar ke lingkungan sekitar dan menyebabkan polusi udara serta menimbulkan kekhawatiran terhadap kesehatan warga dan pengguna jalan.

Kami memohon agar instansi yang berwenang dapat melakukan inspeksi di lokasi tersebut, menghentikan pembakaran sampah secara terbuka, dan bekerja sama dengan desa/banjar setempat untuk menyediakan solusi yang aman dan berkelanjutan bagi pengumpulan, pemilahan, dan pengelolaan sampah.

Saya melampirkan foto/video, tanggal dan waktu kejadian, serta lokasi yang tepat sebagai bukti pendukung.

Terima kasih atas perhatian dan tindak lanjutnya.
Dear Sir/Madam,

I would like to report the repeated open burning of household waste, including plastic waste, at [LOCATION].

The burning occurs regularly at this location, most recently on [DATE and TIME]. The resulting smoke spreads into the surrounding area, causing air pollution and raising health concerns for local residents and road users.

We respectfully request that the relevant authorities inspect the location, stop the open burning of waste, and work with the local village/banjar to provide a safe and sustainable solution for the collection, sorting and management of waste.

I have attached photos/videos, the dates and times of the incidents, and the exact location as supporting evidence.

Thank you for your attention and follow-up.
"Report the fire, not the family."

§ VI · SafetyProtect yourself, and your neighbours.

Most reports pass without incident. But several residents have described being threatened after reporting, including warnings from local figures once enforcement officers had left, and in one case a threat involving immigration. Take these precautions.

  • Do not confront anyone at the fire. Tempers may run high near a burn, and a public argument costs the other person face. Leave, document from a distance, and report later.
  • Do not try to put out a large fire yourself. Call 113 if it is spreading.
  • Use LAPOR!'s Anonymous and Confidential options. Remember that in a small street the agency's visit can still point to you, especially if you are the only household that has complained. Reports from several households are safer and carry more weight.
  • Never post the burner's face, house or name online. Indonesia's Electronic Information and Transactions Law (UU ITE) has been used to bring criminal defamation charges over social-media posts. Send evidence to the authorities, not to Instagram.
  • Go with someone. If you need to visit the banjar or the burner, bring a Balinese neighbour, landlord or colleague, and keep the tone polite and about health. Residents report that a calm request in Bahasa Indonesia from someone with standing in the community goes much further than a complaint from a newcomer. People who live alone may feel safer letting a trusted local make the approach.
  • If you are a foreign resident, keep it personal and factual. Filing your own complaint about smoke affecting your home is ordinary use of a public service. Organising patrols, campaigns or drone surveillance of neighbours can be read differently. If you are unsure, take advice before going further.
  • If you are threatened, do not escalate on the spot. Write down what was said, when and by whom, and keep any messages. Some residents found the local police (Polsek) more responsive than village officials. Foreign residents threatened with immigration action should get advice from an immigration lawyer or their consulate before responding.
  • Remember the other side. The person burning is often your neighbour, with nowhere affordable to take their rubbish. A report that leads to a fine without a working collection service may move the fire rather than stop it. Where you can, pair a report with an offer of help.

What happens next. An officer may visit, warn the household, ask the village to put up signs and clear the burn site, and promise follow-up monitoring. Track your LAPOR! report and add a comment if the burning returns. Repeat reports from the same location are what move a case up the list.

Why the data matters. Each report on its own is a story. Together with the community sensor record, which shows the morning spikes rising and falling across the island, they become evidence that the problem is widespread, persistent and measurable. That is the case regency governments need to fund collection rather than leave it to each banjar.
